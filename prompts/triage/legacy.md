## Step 1: membership and evidence of municipal housing scope

Read the documents together. Identify the municipal decision challenged, its
proposed use, and the permission at stake. Later motions, reopening, or higher
court review retain the underlying appeal's identity. A separate historical
case does not confer its scope on the current matter.

housing=yes requires BOTH facts to be established by the source:
1. The proposed use is residential dwellings or residential building lots.
2. The appeal concerns permission for new, replacement, or additionally authorized
   dwelling units/house lots, including conditions of that permission.
The housing opportunity is the dwelling unit, not its occupants: permission for
different tenants, household composition, or occupancy of an already authorized
dwelling does not add housing. Such a dispute is housing=no unless it also seeks
a new, replacement, converted year-round, or additional authorized dwelling.
Classify the intended use, not the project's ownership, layout, or zoning label.
A subdivision, condominium, planned development, or building envelope does not
by itself establish residential use. If either fact is unestablished, use unclear.

Qualifying housing includes ADUs, new homes on vacant lots, seasonal-to-year-round
conversions, replacement homes without net unit increase, and care facilities with
apartments rather than beds alone. Additional legal authorization can qualify even
if the dwelling physically exists; existing construction does not establish settled
authorization. Use no only when the source establishes a nonhousing subject, or
settled existing housing with no new/replacement permission at stake. Do not import
a historical permit's proposed housing into the current appeal.

reason explains which facts establish scope or remain missing. evidence contains
one or two exact continuous {doc,page,quote} passages, at most 25 words each. For
housing=yes, include evidence of the residential nature of the proposal itself,
not merely the existence of an appeal or references to neighboring homes.
A no on either scope question ends processing; unclear continues to extraction.
Do not guess yes to avoid exclusion: unclear preserves recall without claiming
confirmed housing. Do not guess no to discard poorly documented matters.
