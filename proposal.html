Research and technical proposal · 22 September 2026

From court opinions to housing appeal timelines

Build a traceable database of housing projects, municipal decisions, appeals, issues, and outcomes from the Vermont court PDF collection—and keep every finding inside that source’s limits.

Planning onlyOnly input: court PDF collection~2,600 postings is an estimate, not a case count
The recommendation

Build the evidence spine first.

01
Core design

Connect documents to cases, then cases to projects.

A ruling may cover several dockets; a docket may produce several rulings; a project may have several permit and appeal tracks. Preserve each original grain.

Corpus boundary

Use the court PDFs as the entire input.

Capture every posting and PDF in the library, then classify what each document shows. The database describes qualifying projects observed in this collection; it cannot count appeals that left no opinion here.

Research claim

Describe observed litigation before claiming impact.

PDFs can support an evidence-backed chronology and issue analysis. Appeal-caused construction delay, total appeals statewide, and actual homes built cannot be established from this collection alone.

Recommended target population: projects observed in the court PDF collection that propose new or additional dwelling units and have at least one appeal of a specific municipal land-use decision to the Environmental Division. Retain linked Act 250 proceedings as context, while counting municipal issues separately. Test ambiguous cases before processing the full collection.
What is known

The PDF library defines the observable study population.

The supplied reconnaissance sampled 156 entries and 22 PDFs across 2000–2026. These observations are not prevalence estimates. The live listing exposes titles, displayed dates, division, docket text, and links to rulings. Sources ↗

02
Observed in supplied memo

Many documents per case

Motion rulings, merits decisions, and later orders can share a docket. Counting PDFs inflates case counts.

Observed in supplied memo

Several dockets per document

One decision can cover related proceedings. The document-to-docket link must be many-to-many.

Permanent source limit

Unpublished appeals are invisible

Some filed appeals may leave no PDF in this library. Their number is unknowable from this source, so report counts as “identified in the PDF collection,” never as all filed appeals.

Correction to the initial framing: Vermont law permits the court to consolidate or coordinate same-project appeals; it does not require every municipal and Act 250 appeal to become one lawsuit. An official 2026 study reports consolidation is infrequent. Record these relationships only when supported. Sources ↗

The spot check found a residential-address municipal appeal about conditions on a home business, and a bylaw challenge titled “Development Regulations Appeal.” Neither is automatically a new-housing project. 0–1

Capture the source, then the bytes

Use listing pages only to discover and inventory PDFs, with retrieval time and displayed metadata retained for audit. Download with bounded concurrency, retry/backoff, content-type and size checks. Preserve raw HTML and PDF bytes; use hashes to identify exact duplicates without deleting distinct postings.

Output → posting manifest, fetch log, content-addressed PDFs
2

Measure text quality per page

Record page count, text-layer coverage, character quality, and failures. OCR only pages that need it. Keep native and OCR text separately, with page coordinates when possible. Pilot older documents before choosing an OCR engine.

Output → page text, OCR provenance, quality flags
3

Triage without erasing mixed cases

Classify issuing court, document type, municipal appeal presence, Act 250 presence, project-specific housing relevance, and uncertainty separately. Preserve mixed decisions; associate individual issues with their tracks later.

Output → labels, evidence snippets, review priority
4

Resolve identities deliberately

Normalize modern and legacy docket formats while retaining raw strings. Match by docket first; use municipality, parties, site, permit, and chronology as supporting evidence. Queue ambiguous links for review instead of merging on title or address alone.

Output → stable IDs and typed, scored links
5

Extract facts as claims

Capture municipal decisions, filings, stays, hearings, motions, remands, judgments, Supreme Court review, appellant roles, proposed units, grounds, and disposition. Each claim carries a source page/span and whether a date is stated, inferred, conflicting, or absent.

Output → event and issue claims with provenance
6–7

Adjudicate, then publish views

Review low-confidence, mixed-track, and conflicting records. Materialize one row per appeal, issue, and project episode. Keep unresolved cases and unknown dates visible; export a project history alongside summary tables.

Output → curated database, audit trail, analytic views

A project is an episode, not a PDF.

Use stable IDs and explicit relationship tables. Avoid forcing every ruling into one case-outcome field.

Stays, remands, judgments, Supreme Court appeals; related/coordinated/consolidated edges

Traceability rule: every normalized date, label, unit count, and outcome points to a court PDF page. Conflicting claims coexist until resolved. Model output alone never makes a fact “known.”

Keys and edges

  • posting_id → document_id: retain every listing occurrence even when PDF bytes match.
  • document_docket: many-to-many, with raw and normalized numbers.
  • appeal_decision: identifies the municipal decision actually challenged.
  • project_appeal: stores why dockets belong to one development episode.
  • appeal_relation: coordination, consolidation, remand, or successor proceeding.

Initial inclusion flags

  • Core: municipal appeal of a specific decision about proposed new/additional dwellings.
  • Context: related Act 250 and Supreme Court activity, remands and municipal redecisions.
  • Review: conversion, occupancy, demolition, mixed-use, or enforcement linked to housing.
  • Outside: stand-alone Act 250, regulation-wide challenges, unrelated enforcement, existing homes without added units.
What the database can answer

Separate elapsed time from claimed harm.

Use named clocks and endpoints. A motion denial is not a permit denial; a court judgment is not proof that homes were built.

Model choice

Jev could accelerate bounded judgments after text extraction.

Jev accepts a plain-text or structured state and returns typed Choice, Score, or Noul answers. The input facts need not already be fully structured; questions and output options are typed. The pipeline does not depend on Jev. It cannot read PDFs/images in this proposed flow, and it does not generate source-grounded names or dates. Docs ↗

Adoption gate

  1. Human-code a stratified pilot, including older scans, mixed appeals, and housing edge cases.
  2. Compare optional Jev use with a PDF-only rules or local-classifier baseline on the same held-out records.
  3. Prioritize recall of qualifying municipal housing documents; route uncertain exclusions for review.
  4. Check tokenized cost and data-handling terms before sending court text to an external API.

Current docs list jev-1.13.0, text-only requests, a 64k total token limit, 32k for state plus longest question, and $0.042 per million input tokens. These vendor terms may change. Model docs ↗

Practical build sequence

Prove fragile assumptions before scaling.

This is a proposed sequence, not an execution log. No bulk harvesting or classification was done for this deliverable.

07
Phase A

Definitions + corpus contract

Approve a concise codebook and freeze a complete manifest of PDF postings. Define the observable population, inclusion rules, and missingness labels.

Phase B

Stratified pilot

Sample about 100 postings across decades and likely categories; double-code a subset. Deliver extraction diagnostics and revised inclusion rules.

Phase C

Full ingest + review

Run resumable capture, selective OCR, triage, and project linking. Reconcile every manifest entry to a processed, excluded, or unresolved status.

Phase D

Bounded analysis release

Publish PDF-observed project histories, durations, grounds, appellant roles, stays, and uncertainty. Mark construction outcomes and causal effects as unanswerable from this source.

Pilot exit criteria

  • Every sampled posting has a fetch status; every PDF has a hash and page-level text-quality record.
  • Known municipal housing items are not silently excluded; false negatives are reviewed and triage is retuned.
  • A reviewer can reproduce project links, issue tags, and dates from cited pages.
  • Every discovered PDF posting has a disposition in the processing manifest; unobserved cases are explicitly outside the denominator.

Codebook decisions

  • When conversions, occupancy, enforcement, or amendments count toward added or preserved homes.
  • How to define one project; recommend a versioned development episode.
  • Primary endpoint; recommend reporting Division disposition, last observed legal resolution, and post-remand municipal action separately.
  • Historical changes in law and older-data gaps; stratify by year instead of applying one timeless rule.
